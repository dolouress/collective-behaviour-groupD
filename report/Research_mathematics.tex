Collective behaviour emerges from local interactions among individuals and plays an important role in how animal groups coordinate their movements and respond to external threats. Sheep herding provides a useful setting for investigating these interactions and the propagation of directional information within a group. This project builds on the agent-based sheep-herding model proposed by Jadhav et al. (2024), which describes collective sheep responses to a herding dog through rules governing repulsion, attraction, alignment, and threat avoidance. As an initial step, we reproduce the original computational model in MATLAB and reimplement it in Python. The two implementations are evaluated using established measures of group cohesion, polarization, elongation, and the relative movement of the dog and flock. Preliminary comparisons show similar statistical distributions across the six evaluated measures, supporting the reproducibility of the original model. Building on this baseline, we aim to investigate how modifications to individual interactions or herding strategies influence collective movement and group stability. The planned extension will be evaluated through controlled simulation experiments and quantitative comparisons with the original model.
\end{abstract}
